    Weight decay has a direction, too

    When we tune weight decay, we usually tune its strength: how large should the coefficient be, should it change during training, and should different modules use different values? These questions all assume that the direction of decay is already settled. For matrix weights, it need not be.

    AdamW separates the task update from weight shrinkage. The optimizer follows information from gradients; the decay term acts on the current weights. Ordinary weight decay (WD) points along WWW, but a matrix’s singular-value decomposition gives us other directions to consider.

    On its own, ordinary WD makes every singular value smaller by the same factor. The matrix shrinks, but its relative spectrum stays the same. Can we instead choose which parts of the spectrum to shrink more strongly?

    Spectral-power weight decay, denoted by Sp\mathcal{S}_pSp​ WD, makes this choice through a singular-value power. Let W∈Rm×nW\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}W∈Rm×n have the compact rank-rrr SVD W=Udiag⁡(σ1,…,σr)V⊤W=U\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r)V^\topW=Udiag(σ1​,…,σr​)V⊤, where U,VU,VU,V have orthonormal columns and σi>0\sigma_i>0σi​>0 are the nonzero singular values. Define

    Sp(W)=Udiag⁡(σ1p,…,σrp)V⊤.S_p(W)=U\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^p,\ldots,\sigma_r^p)V^\top.Sp​(W)=Udiag(σ1p​,…,σrp​)V⊤.

    The exponent p≥0p\ge0p≥0 determines how shrinkage varies with mode size. We use Sp(W)S_p(W)Sp​(W) for the decay direction and Sp\mathcal{S}_pSp​ WD for the method. The associated penalty is

    Rp(W)=1p+1∑i=1rσip+1=1p+1∥W∥Sp+1p+1.R_p(W)=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i^{p+1} =\frac{1}{p+1}\lVert W\rVert_{S_{p+1}}^{p+1}.Rp​(W)=p+11​i=1∑r​σip+1​=p+11​∥W∥Sp+1​p+1​.

    Here ∥W∥Sq=(∑iσiq)1/q\lVert W\rVert_{S_q}=(\sum_i\sigma_i^q)^{1/q}∥W∥Sq​​=(∑i​σiq​)1/q is the Schatten norm. For p>0p>0p>0, Sp=∇RpS_p=\nabla R_pSp​=∇Rp​; at p=0p=0p=0, SpS_pSp​ is a nuclear-norm subgradient (Lewis, 1995; Watson, 1992). Ordinary WD is recovered at p=1p=1p=1. Other powers change the relative pressure on the large and small singular values—the head and tail of the spectrum.

    The next question is whether changing the weight spectrum also changes the representations the model learns, and whether those changes help prediction. We will follow this progression from a solvable linear model to Transformer experiments.

    Weights, representations, and loss

    The weight spectrum tells us how parameter magnitude is distributed across singular modes. The activation spectrum tells us what representations those weights produce on the data. Validation loss measures how well the resulting model predicts.

    These are related measurements, not interchangeable ones. Sp\mathcal{S}_pSp​ WD acts on weights rather than the current task gradient, so a change in rank is evidence of a geometric effect—not, by itself, evidence of better learning.

    1. From ordinary weight decay to decoupled optimizers

    1.1 When L2 regularization and weight decay coincide

    Ridge regression and Tikhonov regularization add a quadratic penalty to control parameter size (Hoerl & Kennard, 1970; Tikhonov & Arsenin, 1977):

    min⁡WL(W)+β2∥W∥F2.\min_W L(W)+\frac{\beta}{2}\lVert W\rVert_F^2.Wmin​L(W)+2β​∥W∥F2​.

    Early analyses of weight decay studied how this norm penalty suppresses irrelevant components and limits the effect of noise in the training targets (Krogh & Hertz, 1991).

    1.2 Why the distinction matters beyond plain SGD

    Momentum averages gradients over time (Sutskever et al., 2013); AdaGrad and Adam also rescale coordinates using gradient history (Duchi et al., 2011; Kingma & Ba, 2015). Write the parameters as a vector wtw_twt​, the stochastic task gradient as gtg_tgt​, and the preconditioner as PtP_tPt​. In Adam, the diagonal scaling uses the inverse square root of a second-moment estimate, with a numerical stabilizer. Leaving out momentum averaging for the moment, coupled L2 regularization has the schematic form

    wt+1=wt−ηtαPt(gt+βwt).w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta_t\alpha P_t(g_t+\beta w_t).wt+1​=wt​−ηt​αPt​(gt​+βwt​).

    Decoupled decay instead acts directly on the parameters:

    wt+1=wt−ηtαPtgt−ηtλwt.w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta_t\alpha P_tg_t-\eta_t\lambda w_t.wt+1​=wt​−ηt​αPt​gt​−ηt​λwt​.

    For plain SGD, Pt=IP_t=IPt​=I, and matching λ=αβ\lambda=\alpha\betaλ=αβ recovers the equivalence above. In Adam, a coupled L2 term also enters the moment estimates and is transformed by the preconditioner. Moving it outside the optimizer therefore changes the algorithm, not just the notation. This distinction can also affect generalization (Tang et al., 2025; Zou et al., 2023).

    1.3 A separate branch for decay

    AdamW makes this separation explicit (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019). Throughout the article, we write the decoupled update as

    Wt+1=Wt−ηt[αDt⏟task update+λWt⏟independent weight decay].W_{t+1}=W_t-\eta_t\left[ \underbrace{\alpha D_t}_{\text{task update}} +\underbrace{\lambda W_t}_{\text{independent weight decay}} \right].Wt+1​=Wt​−ηt​​task updateαDt​​​+independent weight decayλWt​​​​.

    Here DtD_tDt​ is the task direction computed from the gradient and optimizer state. The base coefficients α\alphaα and λ\lambdaλ are fixed; ηt\eta_tηt​ schedules both terms. “Decoupled” means that decay bypasses the optimizer’s preconditioning and state updates. It does not require the two terms to have separate schedules.

    Much of the work on this branch concerns decay strength. AdaDecay assigns parameter-wise rates using gradient statistics (Nakamura & Hong, 2019). Adaptive WD adjusts coefficients according to the relative strengths of task and L2 updates (Ghiasi et al., 2023). AlphaDecay assigns module-wise coefficients in LLMs to balance heavy-tailed spectral statistics (He et al., 2025).

    These methods refine how much decay to apply and where to apply it. Parameter-wise scaling can itself change the update’s geometry, so strength and direction are not completely separate. Our question is more specific: can we prescribe shrinkage in the weight matrix’s singular basis?

    1.4 Matrix-aware optimizers make geometry explicit

    K-FAC, Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon already use matrix structure to shape task updates (Gupta et al., 2018; Jordan et al., 2024; Martens & Grosse, 2015; Vyas et al., 2025). Muon offers a particularly simple example: its idealized matrix update preserves the singular directions of a momentum matrix while replacing each nonzero singular value by one (Jordan et al., 2024). Polar Express efficiently approximates this polar-factor computation (Amsel et al., 2026).

    The same kind of matrix operation can be useful in the decay branch, but it acts on a different object. Muon transforms task momentum; Sp\mathcal{S}_pSp​ WD transforms the current weights. One chooses how to follow the task signal, the other how to regularize the parameters.

    2. What ordinary weight decay controls

    2.1 Isolated decay changes scale, not normalized shape

    Set the task update aside. For nonzero W=UΣV⊤W=U\Sigma V^\topW=UΣV⊤, with Σ=diag⁡(σi)\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i)Σ=diag(σi​), an ordinary-WD step gives

    W+=(1−a)W,a=ηλ,W^+=(1-a)W, \qquad a=\eta\lambda,W+=(1−a)W,a=ηλ,

    where η\etaη is the current schedule multiplier and 0≤a<10\le a<10≤a<1. It follows that

    σi+=(1−a)σi,σi+σ1+=σiσ1.\sigma_i^+=(1-a)\sigma_i, \qquad \frac{\sigma_i^+}{\sigma_1^+}=\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_1}.σi+​=(1−a)σi​,σ1+​σi+​​=σ1​σi​​.

    Both the spectral and Frobenius norms shrink, but all singular-value ratios remain unchanged. So do stable rank and entropy effective rank, or eRank, defined below. This is a statement about an isolated decay step. During training, task updates also change singular values and singular vectors; decay can influence the final spectrum through its interaction with those updates (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019).

    2.2 Norm control can still affect learned structure

    Ordinary WD can therefore be associated with low-rank structure even though its isolated step preserves spectral ratios.     3. Spectral-power weight decay: direction, theory, and spectrum shaping

    3.1 Definition and associated penalty

    To move beyond equal relative shrinkage, replace each singular value in the decay direction by a power:

    Sp(W)=UΣpV⊤,Σp=diag⁡(σip),W=UΣV⊤.S_p(W)=U\Sigma^pV^\top, \qquad \Sigma^p=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i^p), \qquad W=U\Sigma V^\top.Sp​(W)=UΣpV⊤,Σp=diag(σip​),W=UΣV⊤.

    Three useful cases are

    S0(W)=UV⊤,S1(W)=W,S2(W)=UΣ2V⊤.S_0(W)=UV^\top, \qquad S_1(W)=W, \qquad S_2(W)=U\Sigma^2V^\top.S0​(W)=UV⊤,S1​(W)=W,S2​(W)=UΣ2V⊤.

    At p=1p=1p=1, this is ordinary WD. At p=0p=0p=0, the active polar factor assigns the same absolute decay to every nonzero mode. At p=2p=2p=2, large modes receive more decay in both absolute and relative terms.     For p>0p>0p>0, this direction is the gradient of a unitarily invariant penalty:

    Rp(W)=1p+1∑iσip+1=1p+1∥W∥Sp+1p+1.R_p(W)=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_i\sigma_i^{p+1} =\frac{1}{p+1}\lVert W\rVert_{S_{p+1}}^{p+1}.Rp​(W)=p+11​i∑​σip+1​=p+11​∥W∥Sp+1​p+1​.

At p=0p=0p=0, the penalty is the nonsmooth nuclear norm, and S0S_0S0​ is one of its subgradients (Cai et al., 2010; Watson, 1992).     Wt+1=Wt−ηt[αDt+λpSp(Wt)].W_{t+1}=W_t-\eta_t\left[ \alpha D_t+\lambda_pS_p(W_t) \right].Wt+1​=Wt​−ηt​[αDt​+λp​Sp​(Wt​)].

Equal numerical values of λp\lambda_pλp​ do not match decay strength across powers.

    4. Case study: Head-wise Muon and V spectral regularization

    4.1 From head-wise to full-matrix V updates

    Public reports for GLM-5, Kimi K3, and Qwen3.8-Next use head-wise Muon on query, key, and value weights (GLM-5 Team, 2026; Kimi Team, 2026; Qiu et al., 2026); DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash instead uses head-wise updates only for query and key (DeepSeek-AI, 2026).

    Use H for head-wise updates and F for full-matrix updates, in Q/K/V order. HHH splits all three projections by head. HHF changes only V, applying Muon to the full matrix spanning all heads. Since Muon orthogonalizes task momentum, changing this grouping changes the task-update geometry. It does not directly make the weights or activations orthogonal.

    Q/K determine where information is read from; V supplies the content being read and aggregated (Vaswani et al., 2017). Whether V heads learn complementary content features therefore deserves separate consideration. Head-wise polar shapes each momentum block separately, while full-matrix polar processes the entire V momentum jointly, including directional overlap across heads. This suggests a natural interpretation of HHF: joint updates may help the heads make fuller use of the available representation space. The learned representations change, while the architecture and parameter count remain the same.

    We first compare HHH with HHF. We then keep the HHF update rule and add spectral decay to V, asking whether a separate adjustment to its weight spectrum helps.

    6. A related extension: spectral projection in the decay branch

    A power law changes shrinkage across the whole spectrum. We can also ask which modes should receive it.     7. Closing thoughts

    Ordinary WD scales all singular values equally in an isolated step. Sp\mathcal{S}_pSp​ WD changes that rule with one exponent: p<1p<1p<1 puts relatively more pressure on weak modes, while p>1p>1p>1 puts more pressure on strong ones. The linear model makes the effect exact; the eight-layer Transformer shows that it can remain visible after task-driven training.

    The case studies show concrete uses for this control. In the V experiment, full-matrix updates learn more spread-out content representations with less cross-head overlap, and further regularization tuning improves the default HHF recipe. In the asymmetric experiment, nearly matching validation loss still accompanies markedly different weight spectra and activation statistics. These results invite a further question: how should mode magnitudes and roles be allocated to make fuller use of a model’s effective representational capacity?

    The useful addition is a way to specify and test spectral preferences rather than leaving them entirely to the optimizer and scalar decay. Powers, coefficients, module allocation, and band selection offer progressively finer choices. Which ones help depends on the task and training regime. Stage-dependent spectral regularization in ORCA (Y. Liu et al., 2026) and quantization-oriented spectral decay in S²D (Chavan et al., 2026) suggest concrete settings in which to study that question.

    Spectrum shaping may also serve as a tool for training stability. Spectral-norm reparameterization has been used to mitigate attention entropy collapse and improve Transformer training stability (Zhai et al., 2023). We conjecture that p>1p>1p>1 or spectral-band selection could target excessively amplified modes, helping control spectral-norm growth, reduce loss spikes, and widen the range of stable learning rates.

    Weight decay need not shrink every mode in the same proportion. Choosing a different shrinkage law gives us control over the spectrum; whether that control is useful has to be answered by the task.
